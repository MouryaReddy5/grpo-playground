GRPO Playground

A policy starts out guessing answers to “a + b = ?” at random. For each question it samples a group of answers, scores them, and pushes up the ones that beat their group's average. That is GRPO, the method DeepSeek used to train reasoning without a value network. Three learners train side by side on identical data.

GRPO advantage: Aᵢ = (rᵢ − mean(r)) / std(r) within each group. REINFORCE uses the raw reward as the advantage, so every sampled answer with positive reward gets pushed up, right or not. The mean-baseline variant subtracts the group mean but skips the std normalization (as in Dr. GRPO).

Setup: the policy is a table of logits, one 10-way softmax for the tens digit and one for the ones digit per question. Each step draws 8 questions, samples G answers each, and takes 2 clipped-ratio updates (ε = 0.2) with a k3 KL penalty to the starting policy, exactly the GRPO objective, minus the neural network. Things to try: “shifted” adds +1 to every reward, which changes nothing about which answer is better. GRPO and the mean baseline don't care, but REINFORCE now pushes up every sampled answer and slows down a lot. With “exact only” and G = 2, most groups are all-wrong and give no signal at all, which is why group size matters for sparse rewards.
